## Part 1

What worked well? (1 paragraph)

In our in class activity, we pasted in our entire question set at once. While doing that provided results quickly, we lost the opportunity to ask follow up questions. We felt that re-doing the interviews again with follow up questions was more effective.

What key problems did you encounter and how did you overcome them? (1 paragraph)

One chief difficulty of the interviews, noted by Cassie,was the difficulty with shepherding the interviewee. But she notes that this is a difficulty even in human interviews. Presumably whoever setup the interview personas prompted them to be "human-like"--removing that from their programming and telling them to be, "Efficient and machine like" in their responses might correct Cassie's issue, but at the cost of "human-similarity."

What worked well? (1 paragraph)

What key problems did you encounter and how did you overcome them? (1 paragraph)

To me (Jacob contributing here) it felt like the bottleneck was "LLM-switching" -- it would have been ideal if the entire team had a single LLM with knowledge of each individual user's local repo, continually updating itself and providing suggestions as changes come in. This will probably need to wait for cheaper tokens.

## Part 3

What worked well? (1 paragraph)

Again the LLM generated prompts were helpful. My LLM helpfully discussed with me the desired features to focus our storyboards on, and identified that our distinguishing competitive feature was the subscription service. We had it output both svgs and ASCIIs, and the stories it generated along with each provided color to our design.

What key problems did you encounter and how did you overcome them? (1 paragraph)

Jacob did this part and noted some reuse of specific branding concepts from Uber--he wishes he had explicitly prompted to avoid reusing verbatim Uber expressions like "X" and "XL" etc. Another thing that we think is a good idea for UX in particular is to ask for **multiple options** for each given interface. In Jacob's experience, usually the first option is best, but often the 2nd or 3rd option is superior, indicating the importance of telling the LLM to come up with alternatives--this replicates human reasoning more closely.
