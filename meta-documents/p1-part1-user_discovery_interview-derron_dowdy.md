## Introduction

Hello! I'm working on a startup called **Ultra**, which is a
ride‑sharing app designed for people underserved by Uber and Lyft. Our
target users include parents needing safe rides for kids, people without
cars who rely on rides to get to work, and riders who want transparent,
affordable pricing.

I'd love to learn about your transportation habits and pain points
through a short interview of about 10 questions. Please share openly ---
there are no wrong answers.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 1 -- Current Transportation

**Interviewer:** How do you currently get around for daily
transportation?

**Participant:**\
People around me use a mix of transportation options. My sister mostly
drives her SUV because she's constantly taking her kids places. A
coworker depends on the train to get into the city, even though it's
often delayed. It really varies depending on someone's lifestyle and
where they live.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 2 -- Taxi Frustrations

**Interviewer:** What frustrations do people experience with taxis in
big cities?

**Participant:**\
Catching a cab can be frustrating. My friend Mark talked about standing
on a street corner for twenty minutes trying to hail a taxi while empty
cars drove past him. Another common complaint is price unpredictability.
My roommate once took a taxi and the fare ended up being almost double
what she expected because of traffic.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 3 -- Uber/Lyft Frustrations

**Interviewer:** What frustrations do people have using Uber or Lyft?

**Participant:**\
Safety concerns come up often. My sister said she sometimes feels uneasy
using rideshare late at night because she worries about driver
background checks. Another big issue is pricing. A neighbor told me the
price jumped from \$15 to \$35 within minutes because of surge pricing,
which made him feel like the app was manipulating the cost.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 4 -- When People Choose a Cab

**Interviewer:** When do people choose a cab instead of driving or using
public transportation?

**Participant:**\
Usually when they're running late or public transportation isn't
reliable. A friend mentioned that she takes a cab when the subway has
signal problems and she needs to get somewhere quickly. My brother also
says people tend to order rides late at night because it feels safer
than walking or waiting for a bus.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 5 -- Price Predictability

**Interviewer:** How important is price predictability versus getting
the lowest fare?

**Participant:**\
People are split on this. Some riders want to know exactly what they'll
pay upfront so they can plan their budget. Others are willing to check
multiple apps and wait longer if it means getting the cheapest ride.
Surge pricing is especially frustrating because people feel tricked when
prices jump unexpectedly.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 6 -- Scheduling Rides

**Interviewer:** How do people feel about scheduling rides in advance
versus requesting on demand?

**Participant:**\
Scheduling rides can be useful for things like airport trips early in
the morning. However, some people don't trust it because drivers aren't
always guaranteed. Many riders prefer requesting rides on demand since
they don't always know exactly when they'll be ready to leave.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 7 -- Safety Features

**Interviewer:** What safety features matter most in ride‑sharing apps?

**Participant:**\
People mention several features. Some like the PIN verification system
so they know they're entering the correct vehicle. Others prefer
real‑time trip sharing so friends or family can monitor their ride.
Transparency about driver background checks is also very important to
riders.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 8 -- Favorite Drivers

**Interviewer:** How would you feel about favoriting or requesting
specific drivers?

**Participant:**\
Some riders would really appreciate that feature. My neighbor said she'd
feel safer if she could request a driver she already trusted. A friend
who works late shifts said having a shortlist of reliable drivers would
give her peace of mind. However, some people worry it could increase
wait times if their preferred driver isn't nearby.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 9 -- Commuter Package

**Interviewer:** What would a commuter package (for example, 20 rides
per month for a flat fee) be worth to you?

**Participant:**\
One coworker calculated that he currently spends around \$12--\$15 per
ride commuting. He said a monthly plan would only be worth it if it
lowered the cost to around \$10 per ride, or roughly \$200 per month.
Another friend said she might pay \$250--\$300 just for predictable
commuting costs without worrying about surge pricing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Question 10 -- Barriers to Adoption

**Interviewer:** If Ultra existed today, what would stop you from using
it?

**Participant:**\
The biggest concern would be driver availability. If there aren't enough
drivers, people won't want to wait a long time for rides. Pricing is
also important. If the transparent pricing ends up being more expensive
than Uber or Lyft, many users might stick with what they already know.
Lastly, the app must be reliable and easy to use.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Closing Questions

**Interviewer:** What feature would most motivate you to try Ultra?

**Participant:**\
Predictable pricing would be a major advantage. Riders want to know the
cost upfront without worrying about sudden surge pricing.

**Interviewer:** Is there anything about ride‑sharing apps we didn't
discuss that feels important?

**Participant:**\
Trust in the company is important. If people don't trust the brand or
the safety measures, they'll probably continue using the apps they
already know.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Storyboard Reaction Summaries

**Storyboard 1 -- Compare Ride vs Public Transit Time**\
The user liked being able to quickly compare travel times between
rideshare and public transit, saying it would help them decide faster
during busy commuting hours.

**Storyboard 2 -- Split Fare Instantly**\
The user thought instant fare splitting would make group outings easier
and eliminate awkward conversations about money after the ride.

**Storyboard 3 -- Request Shared Ride Option**\
The user appreciated the option to share rides to save money, but
expressed concern about longer travel times due to additional stops.

**Storyboard 4 -- Avoid High‑Traffic Routes**\
The user responded positively to optimized routing that avoids heavy
traffic, saying it would make rides feel more efficient and reliable.

**Storyboard 5 -- Book Late‑Night Safe Ride**\
The user strongly valued safety features for late‑night rides,
especially identity verification and ride‑tracking features for friends
or family.
