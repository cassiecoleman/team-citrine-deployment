## Interview Transcript

### Q1: How do you currently get around for daily transportation?

**Persona 8:**
> Since I live in Cardiff, I actually don't have a car of my own. I usually just take the bus or the train to get into the office. It's... well, it's alright, I guess? I spend a lot of my morning commute just listening to my music and trying to get into the right headspace for the day.
>
> I do walk quite a bit too, especially if the weather isn't too rubbish. But yeah, mostly public transport for the daily stuff.

*(Follow-up — persona elaborated further:)*

> Since I moved to this new firm last week, I'm still sort of figuring out the best rhythm. The trains are usually okay — I can sit and listen to my music or just sort of stare out the window — but if there's a delay, I get so flustered. I hate being late, especially when I'm the "new girl" at the office! And the buses can be a bit of a nightmare with the Cardiff traffic. Sometimes I just end up walking if it's not pouring down, just to be safe. It's a bit of a trek, but at least I know exactly how long it'll take.

---

### Context: Introducing Ultra

**Interviewer:** We're building a system to allow you to hail cabs on demand via your phone.

**Persona 8:**
> I've seen people doing that. My friends use those apps all the time, but I've always been a bit hesitant to try them myself. I'm so used to just walking to the bus stop or the station.
>
> What if I press the wrong thing and the car goes to the wrong place? Or if it costs way more than I thought it would? I'm a bit of a numbers person, so I like knowing exactly what's coming out of my bank account before I commit to it. I'd probably need to sit down and read through a whole "how-to" guide or something before I felt brave enough to actually book one on my own!

---

### Q2: Think of your common frustrations with the process of using a cab.

**Persona 8:**
> My biggest thing — and I'm a bit of a stickler for this — is the uncertainty of the cost. Since I work with numbers all day, I like everything to balance. It really stresses me out when I'm sitting in the back of a taxi and I'm just watching that little meter click up and up while we're stuck in traffic. It feels like I'm losing control of my budget right then and there!
>
> I worry about the "newness" of it all. Like, if I use an app and something goes wrong, I always assume I've messed it up somehow. I'd much rather have a clear, step-by-step way to know exactly what I'm doing and exactly what it's going to cost me before I even open the car door.

---

### Q3: What are your common frustrations with Uber/Lyft?

**Persona 8:**
> I don't really use them much. I'm a bit of a creature of habit with my bus and train! But my friends use them when we go out, and watching them try to book a ride actually makes me quite nervous.
>
> The biggest thing that puts me off is how the price just jumps. My friend called it "surge pricing." We were standing outside a restaurant and the price was one thing, then she looked again two minutes later and it had gone up by five pounds! As an accountant, that just feels wrong to me. I like to see the numbers and have them stay put so I can plan my spending.
>
> The apps look so busy. There are cars moving all over a map and so many buttons... I'm always afraid I'll tap the wrong thing and accidentally order a giant van or something and get charged a fortune. It just feels like a lot of pressure to get it right quickly.

---

### Q4: In what circumstances do you find yourself hailing a cab as opposed to driving yourself, walking, or using public transportation?

**Persona 8:**
> Since I don't actually drive, it usually comes down to when my usual routine just falls apart.
>
> If it's absolutely tipping it down with rain — which happens quite a bit here in Cardiff — I really don't want to be trudging to the bus stop and getting my work clothes all soaked. Or if I've stayed a bit late at the office to finish off a spreadsheet and I've missed the regular train... that's when I start getting a bit anxious about getting home safely and quickly.
>
> Sometimes, if I'm meeting friends and I'm already running late — which I hate being — I might think about it then, too. But usually, it's a "break glass in case of emergency" sort of thing for me. I'd much rather stick to my usual walk or the bus if I can help it, just because I know exactly how those work.

---

### Q5: How important is price predictability vs. getting the lowest possible fare?

**Persona 8:**
> It's definitely the predictability. I'm a numbers person through and through! I'd much rather know, right to the penny, what I'm going to pay before I even step foot in the car. Even if it's a little bit more expensive than some "maybe" price, at least I can account for it.
>
> When the price is jumping around — like those surge things — it just makes me feel a bit out of control. It's like trying to balance a spreadsheet where the totals keep changing while you're looking at them! It's incredibly stressful.
>
> I'm the sort of person who likes to sit down, look at all the information, and know exactly what the plan is. If I can't predict the cost, I'll probably just stick to the bus because I know exactly what that ticket costs every single time.

---

### Q6: How do you feel about scheduling rides in advance vs. requesting on-demand?

**Persona 8:**
> I'm definitely a scheduler! The idea of just standing on a curb and hoping a car shows up right when I need it makes me so anxious.
>
> I'm the type of person who likes to have everything lined up. If I know I have a train to catch or a big meeting at the new office, I'd much rather book it the night before. It feels more like a solid plan then. It gives me time to double-check the details and make sure I haven't messed anything up.
>
> Requesting one "on-demand" feels so frantic. Like I'm rushing and might tap the wrong button because I'm in a hurry. Plus, if I schedule it, I assume the price is locked in, right? That would be much better for my peace of mind.

---

### Q7: What safety features matter most to you in a ride-sharing app?

**Persona 8:**
> Safety is a huge one for me, especially if I'm traveling alone or it's late.
>
> The most important thing for my peace of mind would be just knowing exactly who is picking me up. Having the driver's photo, their name, and the car's plate number clearly shown — and maybe some sort of check to make sure they are who they say they are.
>
> I've also heard that some places let you specifically request a woman driver now. I think I'd feel much more comfortable with that, especially coming home from a night out or after a late shift at the office.
>
> Being able to send my location to a friend or my mum while I'm in the car — without it being too complicated to set up — would be lovely. If I can just click one thing and they can see I'm on my way, I'd feel much less "alone" in the back of the car.

---

### Q8: How would you feel about being able to favorite/request specific drivers?

**Persona 8:**
> I would love that! That sounds so much better than just getting a total stranger every single time.
>
> Since I'm a bit of a creature of habit, if I find a driver who is polite, drives safely, and — most importantly — doesn't make me feel nervous, I'd much rather just stick with them. It's like how I have my favorite seat on the bus or the train. It takes the stress out of the whole thing because I already know what to expect.
>
> I think it would also help with that "blaming myself" thing I do. If I know the driver and how they work, I'm less likely to worry that I've messed something up with the booking or the pickup location.

---

### Q9: What would a "commuter package" (e.g., 20 rides/month for a flat fee) be worth to you?

**Persona 8:**
> Now that is something that really speaks to the accountant in me! I love a good fixed cost. It's so much easier to put into my monthly budget than trying to guess a different number every week.
>
> If I'm currently spending maybe five or six pounds a day on the bus and train, that's maybe £120 a month. If a "commuter package" for a car was anywhere around that — or even a little bit more for the convenience of not standing in the rain — I'd be very, very tempted.
>
> Even if it was more like £150 or something, just knowing it's paid and I don't have to think about it or look at my bank balance every time I get home... that would be worth a lot to me. It takes the "risk" out of it. I wouldn't have to worry about surge pricing or anything like that because it's already settled.

---

### Q10: If this product existed today, what would stop you from using it?

**Persona 8:**
> It's probably me! The biggest thing would be the fear of getting it wrong. If I download a new app and it's all flashy maps and buttons I don't understand, I just get so flustered. I'd be worried I'd accidentally book a car to the wrong street or click something that charges me double, and then I'd just sit there blaming myself for being "bad with technology."
>
> If there isn't a really clear, step-by-step guide — like a video or a simple tutorial I can look at before I have to actually use it — I'd probably just stick to my bus. Even if the bus is late, at least I already know how that "system" works.
>
> If it feels "too technical" or if the price isn't 100% clear before I hit 'confirm,' I'd probably just delete it and go back to my walking. I just need it to be predictable.

## Synthesis: Core Repeated Concerns

Across all 10 questions, Persona 8 consistently returned to the same set of anxieties and needs. These represent the strongest design signals from the interview:

### 1. Price Predictability Above All Else
Mentioned in **Q2, Q3, Q5, Q6, Q9, Q10**. This was the single most repeated concern. As a self-described "numbers person" and accountant, the persona is deeply uncomfortable with variable or opaque pricing. Surge pricing is a dealbreaker. Fixed, upfront, locked-in fares are essential — she would pay *more* for certainty over a cheaper but unpredictable fare.

### 2. Fear of Making Mistakes / Tech Anxiety
Mentioned in **Q1 (context), Q2, Q3, Q6, Q8, Q10**. The persona repeatedly expressed fear of "pressing the wrong thing," accidentally booking the wrong car or destination, and blaming herself for errors. This points to a critical UX requirement: the app must be **simple, guided, and forgiving** — with clear confirmation steps, undo options, and minimal cognitive load.

### 3. Strong Preference for Routine and Familiarity
Mentioned in **Q1, Q4, Q6, Q8**. The persona is a self-described "creature of habit" who values knowing exactly what to expect. She favors scheduling over on-demand, preferred drivers over strangers, and her existing bus route over any new system she hasn't learned yet. **Onboarding and first-ride experience are make-or-break.**

### 4. Safety and Personal Security
Mentioned in **Q4, Q7, Q8**. Traveling alone and at night creates anxiety. Key requests: verified driver identity (photo, name, plate), option to request a woman driver, one-tap location sharing with a trusted contact. The "preferred driver" feature also maps to safety — familiarity reduces perceived risk.

### 5. Need for Guided Onboarding
Mentioned in **Q2 (context), Q10**. The persona explicitly asked for a "how-to guide," a "video," or a "simple tutorial." Without clear onboarding, she will not adopt the product regardless of how good it is. This is a hard blocker for this user segment.

### Key Takeaway for Ultra
Persona 8 represents a high-value target user (daily commuter, no car, willing to pay £150/month) who will **never convert** without: (1) guaranteed flat pricing, (2) a dead-simple UI with guided onboarding, and (3) the ability to build routine through scheduling and preferred drivers. If Ultra nails these three things, this persona switches from "break glass in case of emergency" cab usage to a daily subscriber.
